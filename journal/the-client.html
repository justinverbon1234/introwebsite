Design and development

The Client

Hi friends, long time no see. For the past three weeks, I've been working on an assignment called “The Client.” As the name already suggests, we got to work with a real client this time, which was a nice change from the usual school assignments.

Written by Justin Verbon Thought / 02

Our client was HvanA, a news website that mainly focuses on students in Amsterdam. They came to us with a straightforward assignment: optimize their website to increase traffic and, where possible, improve user retention. Basically, make the website more appealing, easier to navigate, and give people a reason to stick around.

Sounds simple enough, right? Well, there's quite a lot that goes into something like this, especially when you're working with an existing website that already has its own identity.

Anyways, let's get into it. I'll start with how week 1 went.

Week 1

The first week mainly consisted of meeting the client and getting a better understanding of what he wanted from us. We got introduced to HvanA, what they do, and what they were hoping to achieve with this project.

One thing that became pretty clear was that we weren't just supposed to make something that looked nice. The redesign had to serve a purpose. People should be able to find what they're looking for more easily, and the website should feel appealing enough for them to explore more than just one article.

Besides getting to know the client, we also spent quite a bit of time experimenting with different ideas and figuring out what our final product could eventually look like.

This mainly meant looking at the existing website, seeing what worked and what didn't, and thinking about how we could improve things without completely losing the identity HvanA already had.

I tried out a few different approaches during this stage. Nothing was really set in stone yet, which was quite nice because it gave us the freedom to experiment without worrying too much about whether everything would work in the final design.

By the end of the week, I had a better idea of the direction I wanted to take, so it was time to start building something.

Week 2

Week 2 was mainly about actually building the thing we had been planning. Since we had to start from scratch, there was quite a lot to do.

One of the first decisions I made was to focus on a mobile-first design. And the reasoning behind that was simple: pretty much everyone is on their phone nowadays, especially the younger audience HvanA is trying to reach.

Besides that, I wasn't really a fan of how the original website looked on mobile. There was a lot going on, and it didn't always feel like everything had its own place. Some sections took up quite a bit of space, which meant you had to scroll a lot before getting to the next piece of content.

So instead of designing everything for desktop and then trying to make it work on a smaller screen, I figured it would make more sense to do things the other way around.

One of the bigger changes I made was reducing the number of columns shown on the page. Originally, HvanA displayed over 20 columns, which meant you had to scroll quite a bit to get through everything.

I decided to bring that number down to just three. The idea is simple: whenever a new column gets published, it automatically takes the first spot, while the oldest of the three moves into the catalogue, where you can still find it through the filtering system.

This way, the newest content always gets the attention it deserves, without having to scroll endlessly. I also think it makes the whole experience a lot calmer, because having 20 different articles thrown at you at once can be overwhelming.

And it's not like any content gets lost. It's all still there, just a little better organized.

I mainly wanted to make the website feel a bit more organized and easier to navigate. Something where you could quickly see what's available without having to scroll endlessly or search through a bunch of different sections.

Of course, there was also the challenge of keeping HvanA's identity intact. I didn't want to completely change the website into something unrecognizable. The colors, for example, are part of what makes HvanA, HvanA. So, I wanted to keep those while finding a way to use them a little differently.

Another thing I tried to focus on was making the content itself stand out more. After all, it's a news website, so the articles should be the main attraction, not everything surrounding them.

This meant experimenting with layouts, spacing, typography, and how different articles could be presented. Quite a bit of trial and error, but that's usually how these things go.

By the end of the week, I had gone from a bunch of ideas to an actual design that I could show. It wasn’t perfect yet, but at least there was something to work with.

So yeah, it was a pretty busy week.

Week 3

Week 3 mainly focused on looking back at what we had done so far, figuring out what went well and what could still be improved.

Since we had our review scheduled for Thursday (08-10-2026), we also spent some time making final adjustments and preparing to present what we had been working on.

One thing I noticed while looking back at the design was that even though individual sections might look good on their own, that doesn't automatically mean everything works well together.

And that's something that came up during the review as well.

The review itself went well! We got quite a few useful tips on how we could improve our initial designs. One of the suggestions was to make the different elements blend a bit better, so the whole website feels more connected rather than having a bunch of separate sections.

Which, looking back at it, makes sense.

When you're designing something, it's easy to get caught up in making one section look good. But you also must think about how that section connects to everything around it. Otherwise, you end up with a website where everything looks fine individually, but the overall experience feels a little disconnected.

There were also some things that worked well, so it's not like we have to start over again. It's more about taking what we already have and improving it.

Luckily, this isn't the end of the project just yet. We still have another sprint ahead of us, which means there's plenty of time to take the feedback we received and do something with it.

I'm quite curious to see how much the design will change between now and the final version.

What's next?

So, that's pretty much how the first three weeks went.

Looking back, I think the biggest thing I've learned so far is that designing for an actual client is quite different from just making something for yourself.

Normally, you can pretty much do whatever you want, but with a client, you must consider what they're trying to achieve, who their audience is, and whether your design actually helps with that.

And while I do enjoy having complete creative freedom, having those limitations also makes things a bit more interesting. You must think more carefully about the decisions you're making instead of just going with whatever looks good.

For the next sprint, I'll mainly be focusing on improving the overall consistency of the design, making the different sections work better together, and taking another look at the feedback we received.

There's still some work to do, but I'm pretty happy with where things are heading.

Anyways, that's it for now. See you in the next one, hugs and kisses xoxo.
